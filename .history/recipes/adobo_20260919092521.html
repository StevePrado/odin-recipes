Adobo Recipe

Adobo is an iconic, savory, and tangy dish and cooking method where meat, seafood, or vegetables are marinated and simmered in a rich blend of vinegar, garlic, and soy sauce

What is Adobo

Core Flavors: Characteristically salty, sour, savory, and garlicky, often balanced with whole peppercorns and bay leaves.

Main Ingredients: Usually made with chicken, pork, or a combination of both, slowly braised until tender.

Serving: Almost always served hot alongside a generous portion of white rice to absorb the flavorful reduction sauce.

Ingredients

  • Pork Belly (Liempo): 500g, cut into bite-sized chunks.
  • Chicken Pieces: 500g, bone-in thighs or drumsticks work best for flavor.
  • Garlic: 1 whole head, crushed or coarsely minced.
  • Soy Sauce: 1/2 cup (preferably Filipino brands like Silver Swan or Marca Piña).
  • White Vinegar: 1/3 cup (preferably cane vinegar like Datu Puti).
  • Water or Broth: 1 cup, to help simmer and tenderize the meat.

Steps

  1. Marinate the Meat (30 mins - Overnight)
    • Combine ingredients: In a large bowl, mix the chicken, pork, crushed garlic, soy sauce, whole peppercorns, and bay leaves.
    • Chill: Cover and let it marinate in the refrigerator for at least 30 minutes (overnight yields the deepest flavor).
  2. Sear the Meat (10 mins)
    • Separate meat: Remove the chicken and pork from the marinade, scraping off excess garlic. Reserve the liquid marinade.
    • Brown the pork: Heat oil in a large pot over medium-high heat. Sear the pork chunks until browned on all sides, then remove them.
    • Brown the chicken: In the same pot, sear the chicken pieces skin-side down until golden brown, then remove them.
  3. Simmer and Tenderize (25-30 mins)
    • Combine liquids: Pour the reserved marinade and water (or broth) into the pot. Bring to a boil.
    • Add pork: Return the pork to the pot, lower the heat, cover, and simmer for 15 minutes (pork takes longer to soften than chicken).
    • Add chicken: Put the chicken pieces back into the pot. Cover and simmer for another 15 minutes until both meats are completely tender.
  4. Sauce Reduction & Crisping (10 mins)
    • Pour vinegar: Pour the vinegar into the pot. Crucial step: Let it boil uncovered for 3 to 5 minutes without stirring so the raw alcohol taste cooks off.
    • Balance flavor: Taste the sauce. If it is too sour or salty, stir in the optional sugar to balance it out.
    • Reduce sauce: Continue simmering uncovered until the sauce thickens and reduces to your liking.
    • Optional finish: For a traditional touch, pull the meat out one last time and quickly fry it in a separate pan with a splash of the adobo oil until the edges are crispy, then toss it back into the sauce.
